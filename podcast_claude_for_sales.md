## [SEGMENT 2: PROSPECTING & RESEARCH — 7:00–14:00]

**HOST:**
The first place Claude supercharges sales is prospecting — specifically, account research and building an ICP.

**ICP Development**

If you haven't nailed your Ideal Customer Profile, Claude can help you build one from scratch or sharpen an existing one.

You can prompt Claude like this:

> *"I sell [product/service]. My best customers tend to be [describe 2–3 current customers]. Help me define a detailed ICP including industry, company size, job titles, pain points, and buying triggers."*

Claude will generate a structured profile you can immediately share with your team. You can iterate on it — "make it more specific to mid-market SaaS" or "add a section on what disqualifies a prospect."

**Account Research**

Before any outreach or discovery call, you need context. Claude can compress hours of research into minutes.

Try this prompt:

> *"Here's the LinkedIn profile and company about page for [prospect name] at [company]. What are their likely business challenges? What industry pressures is their company facing? How might my solution — which does [X] — be relevant to them?"*

You paste in the content, Claude synthesizes it. You walk into every conversation prepared.

**Building Prospect Lists**

Claude can help you define the right search criteria for tools like LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Apollo, or ZoomInfo.

> *"I'm targeting VP of Operations at manufacturing companies with 200–500 employees in the Midwest. What Boolean search string should I use in LinkedIn Sales Navigator? What job titles might also hold this role?"*

That's the kind of thinking that used to require a senior rev ops person. Now it takes 30 seconds.

## [SEGMENT 3: COLD OUTREACH THAT ACTUALLY GETS REPLIES — 14:00–22:00]

**HOST:**
Alright, this is the segment most of you probably tuned in for — using Claude to write cold emails and LinkedIn messages that don't get ignored.

Here's the truth about cold outreach: most of it fails because it's generic. It talks about the seller, not the buyer. Claude helps you flip that.

**The Research-First Email**

Here's a framework I call "Research, Relevance, Request" — and Claude can execute all three.

Start with research. Give Claude context about your prospect:

> *"Here is the LinkedIn profile of [name], their company's recent press release about expanding into Europe, and their job posting for a Head of Revenue Operations. What are the 2–3 most likely pain points I should address in a cold email?"*

Claude identifies the angles. Then you move to relevance:

> *"Now write a cold email from me — [your name], [your title] at [your company] — that leads with the European expansion challenge, connects it to how we help companies scale their sales operations internationally, and ends with a low-friction CTA."*

You get a draft in seconds. You edit for your voice. Done.

**The Personalization at Scale Problem**

Here's where Claude gets really powerful: you can build a template system with Claude that lets you personalize at volume.

Create a base prompt template:

> *"Write a cold email for a prospect who is a [title] at a [industry] company with [X employees]. Their company recently [trigger event]. My product helps [ICP] achieve [outcome] by [key mechanism]. Use a conversational tone, keep it under 150 words, and end with a yes/no question."*

Then fill in the variables per prospect. You get personalized emails in 20 seconds each, not 20 minutes.

**Subject Line Testing**

Claude can generate 10 subject line variations in different styles — curiosity-driven, benefit-led, name-drop, question-based — in one shot.

> *"Generate 10 subject lines for this email. Include: 2 curiosity-based, 2 that lead with the outcome, 2 that reference [trigger event], 2 that ask a question, and 2 that are ultra-short (under 5 words)."*

A/B test them. Let data pick winners.

**LinkedIn Outreach**

The same approach applies to LinkedIn DMs. Keep them shorter — under 5 sentences — and always personalize the opening line.

> *"Write a LinkedIn connection request message for [prospect]. Reference their post about [topic from their feed]. Keep it under 300 characters and don't pitch anything."*

That last instruction matters. Claude will follow it.

## [SEGMENT 4: DISCOVERY CALL PREP — 22:00–28:00]

**HOST:**
Let's talk about discovery — arguably the most important part of the sales process, and one where preparation pays massive dividends.

Claude can be your pre-call coach.

**Building a Call Prep Brief**

Before any discovery or demo call, give Claude everything you know about the prospect and ask for a prep brief:

> *"I have a discovery call tomorrow with [name], [title] at [company]. Here's what I know: [paste LinkedIn, company info, previous email thread, any notes]. Generate a call prep brief including: their likely business priorities, the key pain points to explore, 5 discovery questions tailored to their situation, potential objections and how to handle them, and how our product is most likely to be relevant to them."*

You get a brief that would have taken 45 minutes to compile in about 90 seconds.

**Discovery Question Bank**

Claude can build you a custom question bank for any persona or industry:

> *"Generate 20 discovery questions for a VP of Sales at a mid-market B2B SaaS company. Focus on understanding their current sales process, pipeline visibility challenges, rep onboarding friction, and quota attainment."*

Organize these into categories — situational, problem, implication, need-payoff — and you've got a reusable playbook.

**Objection Prep**

Ask Claude to steelman the prospect's objections before you walk in:

> *"What are the 5 most common objections a [prospect type] would raise when evaluating [your type of solution]? For each one, give me a reframe and a follow-up question I can use to understand the objection more deeply before responding."*

This is like sparring before the big match.

## [SEGMENT 5: PROPOSALS, FOLLOW-UPS & CLOSING — 28:00–36:00]

**HOST:**
Now we're in the deal — the prospect is engaged, you've had great discovery, and it's time to move toward a decision.

**Writing Proposals**

Claude can draft proposal narratives that speak directly to the prospect's pain points. Give it your discovery notes:

> *"Based on this discovery call transcript [or notes], write a proposal narrative for [company]. Lead with their primary pain around [X], explain how we solve it with [Y], quantify the expected impact using [their numbers], and close with a clear recommended next step."*

Claude doesn't replace your proposal template — it fills it with language tailored to *this* buyer.

**Follow-Up Emails**

The fortune is in the follow-up, as they say. Claude can write every follow-up in your sequence.

After a call:

> *"Write a follow-up email for a discovery call I just had with [name]. Key takeaways: [3 bullet points]. Next step: [demo in two weeks]. Recap the pain points in their language, confirm the next step, and attach value by mentioning [relevant case study or insight]."*

After sending a proposal:

> *"Write a gentle check-in email, 5 days after sending a proposal to [name]. Don't be pushy. Offer a useful insight — something about [their industry trend] — and reopen the conversation naturally."*

**Handling Objections in Writing**

Sometimes prospects respond with "we're not ready" or "the price is too high" via email. Claude can help you craft a thoughtful response:

> *"A prospect just replied saying 'We don't have budget right now.' Write a response that acknowledges this honestly, asks a clarifying question to understand the timeline, and keeps the door open without being desperate."*

**Closing Language**

When it's time to ask for the business, language matters. Claude can help you craft confident, clear closing language:

> *"Write a closing email for a deal where we've had 3 meetings, they're aligned on value, and I need to drive a decision this week due to end-of-quarter pricing. Strike a balance between urgency and integrity — don't be manipulative."*

That last instruction — "don't be manipulative" — is important. Claude respects it and produces better, more trustworthy language as a result.

## [SEGMENT 6: SALES ENABLEMENT & TEAM USE CASES — 36:00–41:00]

**HOST:**
If you're a sales leader, Claude isn't just a tool for individual reps — it's a force multiplier for your entire team.

**Building Playbooks**

Claude can turn your tribal knowledge into documented playbooks:

> *"I'm going to describe how our best reps handle the discovery process. [Describe it.] Turn this into a structured playbook with sections for pre-call prep, opening the call, key questions, transitioning to demo, and next steps."*

**Training & Roleplay**

Claude can simulate buyer personas for roleplay practice:

> *"Act as a skeptical VP of Finance at a 300-person manufacturing company evaluating a new ERP system. I'm going to pitch you. Push back on pricing, ask hard ROI questions, and bring up a competitor. Stay in character."*

This is like having an on-demand sales coach available 24/7.

**Call Scoring Frameworks**

Give Claude your sales methodology and ask it to build a scoring rubric:

> *"Based on MEDDIC, create a call scoring rubric I can use to evaluate recorded discovery calls. Include what 'good' looks like for each element and what red flags to watch for."*

**Win/Loss Analysis**

Paste in notes from closed-won and closed-lost deals:

> *"Here are notes from 5 deals we won and 3 we lost this quarter. Identify patterns in the wins, patterns in the losses, and give me 3 process improvements our team should consider."*

That's strategic analysis that used to require a consultant. Now it's a prompt.

## [SEGMENT 7: TIPS FOR GETTING THE MOST OUT OF CLAUDE — 41:00–44:00]

**HOST:**
Before I wrap up, here are five principles for using Claude effectively in sales:

**1. Context is everything.**
Claude is only as good as the information you give it. Paste in the prospect's LinkedIn, their company page, the email thread, your notes. The more context, the better the output.

**2. Be specific about your constraints.**
Tell Claude the word count, the tone, the format, the audience. "Write a cold email" gives you a generic result. "Write a 120-word cold email in a direct, no-fluff tone for a CFO who values data" gives you something useful.

**3. Iterate, don't accept the first draft.**
The first response is a starting point. Say "make it shorter," "make it more casual," "add a P.S. line," "rewrite the opening." Claude gets better with each round of feedback.

**4. Use it to think, not just to write.**
Don't just ask Claude to write things. Ask it to analyze, challenge, brainstorm, and stress-test. "What are the weaknesses in this proposal?" is a powerful prompt.

**5. Keep your voice.**
Edit Claude's output so it sounds like you. The goal is to save time, not to sound like a robot. Your authentic voice is still your biggest competitive advantage.
